
# Cover Letter Guide

## Purpose and Character

The cover letter is the primary human-facing artifact of the proposal. Its job
is to establish fit quickly and invite a conversation — not to summarise the
CV, list skills, or demonstrate thoroughness.

Upwork clients read many proposals. The first two sentences determine whether
they keep reading. Lead with the strongest signal of fit, not with
introductory preamble.

## Form Rules

- **Flowing prose only.** No headers, no bullet points, no bold emphasis.
- **Paragraph length:** 3–5 sentences per paragraph. Four paragraphs is typical.
- **Register:** Formal but not stiff. "I would like" not "I want". First person
  throughout. No "I am pleased to apply" type openers.
- **Length:** Short enough to read in under a minute. If it feels long, it is.
- **Do not summarise the CV.** The cover letter makes an argument; the CV
  provides evidence.

## Structure

**Paragraph 1 — Hook and positioning**
State who you are in terms of the domain, not the technology. Lead with the
thing most relevant to this specific posting. If there is a differentiator
(e.g. Symfony+WordPress integration for a WordPress/PHP API posting), it
belongs here.

**Paragraph 2 — Specific evidence**
One or two concrete examples from relevant projects. Name the capability
demonstrated, not the client or employer. Keep it grounded — specific beats
impressive.

**Paragraph 3 — Fit signal or secondary evidence**
Either a second angle of fit, or a brief statement about approach/working
style if relevant to what the posting signals it cares about.

**Paragraph 4 — Close**
Brief. Invite a conversation. If the user is new to Upwork and pricing low,
this is where "I am new to Upwork and looking forward to building my presence
on the platform" belongs — as a natural aside, not a disclaimer. Do not close
with hollow phrases like "I look forward to the opportunity to contribute."

## What Goes Where

**In the cover letter:**
- Strongest evidence of fit
- Honest acknowledgment of level mismatch *only* if load-bearing (e.g. GeekPay)
- Brief Upwork-new line if rate is below experience level
- Invitation to discuss

**Not in the cover letter:**
- Milestone structure or contract terms → use Upwork's dedicated terms field
- Rate or budget discussion → let the submitted rate speak
- Apologies or disclaimers for gaps → either address the gap directly or omit
- Lists of skills or technologies

## Gap Handling

Honest gaps should be acknowledged in the cover letter only when they are
load-bearing — i.e. when a thoughtful client would notice and the explanation
makes the candidacy stronger rather than weaker (as with GeekPay's junior
framing).

Minor gaps (e.g. a secondary technology the posting lists but doesn't centre)
are usually better left unmentioned. Ask the user if unsure whether a gap
rises to the level of requiring acknowledgment.

## Milestone Strategy

When a posting has an unclear scope or a budget anomaly, recommend a discovery
milestone rather than the completed project. A good first milestone is:

> **Integration assessment and implementation plan** — a short written
> deliverable covering what APIs are involved, what the connection points are
> in the existing application, any obstacles or prerequisites, and a clear
> description of the work to be done.

This framing belongs in Upwork's dedicated milestone/terms field — a sentence
or two explaining that you'd like to begin with a scoped discovery phase before
proceeding to implementation. It does not belong in the cover letter.

## Upwork Proposal Context

Upwork's own guidance on strong proposals:
- Capture attention in the first two sentences
- Demonstrate you've read and understood the posting
- State how you'll solve their problem
- Highlight USPs — why you rather than someone else
- Include a clear call to action

The cover letter for this user does not follow the bullet-heavy Upwork template
literally, but it should satisfy these goals in prose form.
